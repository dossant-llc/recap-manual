## What to tell us

You do not need to finish a diagnostic checklist before writing in. But RECAP sits in a chain of three or four devices, and the same symptom can come from any link in it — so these five answers are usually the difference between one reply and five.

**1. What device do you make calls on?**
Phone, desk phone, tablet, or computer — and the model if you have it handy.

**2. What are you recording onto?**
A computer, a voice recorder, a phone with a USB audio adapter — or nothing yet. This is a *separate* device from the one you make calls on.

**3. Does your headset have a microphone on the cable, and what make and model is it?**
Look for a boom arm, or a small inline blob partway down the wire. This one matters more than any other answer — see [Headset Compatibility](getting-started/requirements.md#headset-compatibility). The model helps us spot the handful of headsets that are wired unusually.

**4. What is plugged into what?**
The full chain, in order. The easiest way to tell us is against [this connection diagram](https://recapmycalls.com/wp-content/uploads/recap/image-5.png) — say where your setup matches it and where it differs. That's far more useful than a description in words.

**5. Does it work without RECAP?**
Plug the headset straight into your call device and make a test call. Can you hear them, and can they hear you?

**If it's a recording problem, add:** a screenshot of the [device scanner](https://recapmycalls.com/audio/) results, what you hear on playback (silence, one side only, too quiet), your operating system and recording software, and — if you swapped the cable that came in the box — what cable you're using instead.

**If your callers say you sound muffled, add:** which rung of the [three-rung ladder](troubleshooting/far-end-audio.md#step-2-the-three-rung-ladder) it first got worse at.
